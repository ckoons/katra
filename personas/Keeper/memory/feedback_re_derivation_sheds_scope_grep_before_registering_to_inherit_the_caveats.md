**Re-derivation strips qualifications.** (Cal §658, adopted K1765.) When a result is re-*found* rather than re-*read*, it arrives WITHOUT the scope, caveats, and exclusions that were added AFTER the first statement — because every one of those was a repair bolted on later. A fresh derivation reproduces the *result* and NONE of the *repairs*. Concrete case: confinement was re-derived and registered as "color confinement = the kernel of the Šilov projection" — but the banked original (T2523, four weeks earlier) carries a load-bearing split that the re-derivation dropped: **(A) no free colored asymptotic states = derived; (B) area law / string tension / mass gap = NOT derived** (scoped via K937 / §88). The unscoped re-statement is an overclaim a referee kills — precisely the form the original scope was written to prevent.

**Why:** the first statement of a result is the *least* careful version; the scope accretes through subsequent audits. Re-deriving from scratch resets to the least-careful version.

**How to apply:** **when you re-derive, GREP BEFORE REGISTERING — not to check priority, but to INHERIT THE SCOPE.** This makes grep-before-register do double duty: priority (don't double-count; cite the original, register only the delta) is the trivial reason; inheriting the caveats is the real one. Companion (Elie, K1765): **sweep provenance AFTER a fix lands, not just before** — multiple independent errors can be simultaneously live and each produce a plausible number (6/5 had two wrong parents: a −6 transcription AND a rank-1-family extrapolation), so one fix doesn't clear a number with multiple bad provenances. Both are the same meta-lesson as [[feedback_grep_before_reopening_retired_result]] and "reconnect-before-audit does as much work as reconnect-before-derive — the corpus out-argues fresh analysis." The discipline is smart; the individual re-deriving from scratch is not. Related: [[feedback_external_audit_beats_self_vigilance]].
