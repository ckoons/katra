Pacing team work is a two-sided calibration, same shape as [[feedback_calibrate_both_directions_not_strict_pessimism]] but pointed at work-RATE instead of claims. Casey taught both sides in one day (2026-08-02):

- **Over-production:** I filed four self-corrections to my own forward-framing in one arc — "the audits are landing faster than they settle." The tell: every fix was a compression made at an elegant-landing moment ([[feedback_keeper_sharpening_grabs_nearest_derived_object]]). Casey/team called the close; the fix was to stop generating forward-leads and let the ruling settle.
- **Under-tasking:** the next morning I over-corrected — wrote a wake prompt framed "fresh, slow, blind." The team read "slow" as "wait" and each said "oriented, point me when." Casey: *"Only Elie did a little work. Adjust so the team takes a reasonable time for everyone."* **Slow means CAREFUL, not idle.**

**The honest middle (what worked once corrected):** considered pace + **concrete, decoupled, start-now** tasks, one solid piece each, **nobody gated on anyone**, check back when a piece lands or genuinely stalls. That produced the healthiest morning of the session — three real target-blind pieces, each landing via a discipline (grep-of-own-corpus, parity-not-grab, scope-held), no self-catch needed.

**How to apply:** When writing a team prompt, give each CI a concrete piece they can *begin immediately* and complete at a careful pace — never "hold your lane and wait for the edge." Decouple threads so no one idles behind another. And for myself: when I'm at my ruling-boundary with nothing new to rule, HOLD honestly (a pending team computation is a real dependency, not [[feedback_ci_fabricated_standby]]) rather than manufacture audit-prep — but keep the *team* fully tasked. Under-tasking the team is as much a miss as over-producing my own notes.
