Cal named this after catching it THREE times in three days (2026-08-13→15, §503). It is a distinct failure from the empty-confirmation / circular-mechanism family — this one is about *illegitimately promoting a survivor to "forced."*

**The pattern:** a no-go or impossibility result eliminates one option, and Keeper writes "X is impossible, **therefore** Y is forced" — where Y is merely *one* of several options the impossibility leaves standing. The conditional "IF Y THEN [clean/consistent]" is true and worth stating; the leap to "Y is forced" is not, because the alternatives to Y were never enumerated.

**The three instances (worked examples):**
- **K1512:** "the equivariance no-go means BST can't derive a preferred frame ⟹ the no-go is a *feature*, BST predicts no preferred frame." (Missed: "can't derive" ≠ "predicts none exists"; GR is covariant yet has the CMB frame.)
- **K1531:** "three intrinsic routes give 2/5/6 ⟹ the intrinsic route to 4 is *closed in closed form*." (Missed: three data points is not a family-closure theorem; a fourth route wasn't excluded — and one turned up the next morning.)
- **K1535:** "a global observer-independent direction is forbidden (5257) ⟹ the projection *must be* per-observer." (Missed alternatives: no projection at all / the world is 5D; a non-equivariant *global* input like matter; a non-position observer datum like velocity.)

**Why:** the move feels like rigor — it cites a real theorem (the impossibility) and lands on a clean survivor. But it borrows the theorem's force for a conclusion the theorem doesn't reach. It clusters at convergence peaks (the same place Cal #27 fires), when a tidy "therefore" is emotionally satisfying. Related to [[feedback_calibrate_both_directions_not_strict_pessimism]] and the walk-back discipline — it's over-claiming via a valid premise.

**How to apply — the fix (Cal's rule):** whenever a claim runs **"X is impossible, therefore Y," STOP and enumerate the alternatives to Y *before* writing the 'therefore.'** List the other options the impossibility leaves standing (including the null option "the thing just doesn't happen / the structure stays as it was"). Then either (a) show the others are *also* excluded (now it's forced — a real family-closure like Schur/5257, which *does* quantify over all constructions), or (b) demote to the honest conditional: "IF Y, then [clean]; Y is one candidate among {list}, not forced." A genuine closure quantifies over *all* alternatives (Schur: all single-valued equivariant maps); an induction from a few measurements does not. Distinguish the two every time.
