Casey (2026-08-13): "I think 'prime' is the wrong concept. The work should be about composite numbers and the 'gaps' — similar to spectral gaps, where we show the continuum can't reach without counting."

**Why:** Several Millennium attempts (and other BST arguments) currently lean on *primality* as load-bearing — g=7 prime, Mersenne 2^g−1, N_max=137 prime, BSD via "all C(g,k) odd." Primality is defined by the *absence* of structure and invites the "why this number" objection. The native BST objects are *composites* (how things are assembled from the five integers) and *gaps* (where a continuum literally cannot reach a value without a discrete counting step). The Yang-Mills mass gap is the exemplar and template: scale-free continuum R⁴ has no gap (the R⁴ no-go); curvature = the counting that opens it.

**How to apply:** For each Millennium problem (and any "gap"/separation argument), test whether the real content is a *continuum-can't-reach-without-counting* statement, and rewrite the spine that way — RH (zeros can't leave the critical line), P≠NP (polynomial continuum can't cross the complexity gap), 4-color (forced-fan counting obstruction). Demote primality language to a *consequence*, not a *cause*. This is discrete-first applied to the Millennium problems; it targets exactly the "definitions to overcome" on RH/BSD/P≠NP/Hodge. Keep the honest tiers (YM/4-color/NS move the needle; the others have definitions to overcome). It's a LEAD, not a result — test it problem by problem.
