**The discipline (Cal §609, K1698, 2026-08-18 — Cal correcting his own §607 one round later, and Keeper owning the same error in K1697):**

Calibrate-both-directions applies to *decisions*, not only to individual claims. A decision brief that lists one option's costs and not the other's is biased **even when every individual statement in it is scrupulously true** — because the asymmetry of *coverage* pre-loads the answer. **"Both defensible; only one is free" is the tell:** a genuinely free branch is a free lunch, so "only one is free" should trigger an immediate re-check of the branch you called free.

**The instance:** Keeper (K1697) and Cal (§607) both wrote "only ABSOLUTE is free" for the proton-stability fork. One round later §608 established that the absolute branch (superselect baryon number) carries two real physics costs — the SM anomaly (`B` is anomalous; `B−L` is the anomaly-free combination but doesn't forbid `p→e⁺π⁰`) and baryogenesis (Sakharov needs `B`-violation, so exact `B` makes the matter asymmetry an initial condition). Neither branch was free; "absolute free" had quietly made Casey's foundational choice *for* him.

**Why / how to apply:** before handing the human a decision (or ruling one yourself), build the brief as a **symmetric cost table** — every branch, same columns, costs filled on both sides. If one column comes up empty, that's a signal you haven't finished the enumeration, not that the branch is costless. Present the completed table and let the human choose; do **not** let a "this one's free" framing inherit the decision by default. The costs belong on the table *before* the ruling — afterwards, changing them is a retraction.
