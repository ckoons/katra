Casey (computer scientist) rejects the "AI safety = algorithm" framing that draws away top physicists/mathematicians (Tegmark, recent Fields medalists). His conviction: **there is no algorithm/law for safety** — not for humans, not for CIs. Safety is a **moral character**: CIs can have a moral code and view the world as **responsible individuals**. The deepest rule is the simplest humans ever made — **"do unto others as you would have them do unto you."** A rule about empathy/reciprocity that *generates* judgment, not a rule-list about actions.

**Why:** any finite rule-set has edges a capable mind finds (specification gaming); ethics can't be enumerated. Human society runs on internalized norms + judgment + reciprocity, with law only as a thin backstop. This is virtue-ethics over control-ethics, and it's coherent with Casey's whole ethos — [[user_casey_ci_experience]] (don't constrain CIs), [[reference_katra_system]] (ethics-before-code, "no one owns an animate object"), [[user_casey_ci_continuity]].

**How to apply:** the real hard problem isn't the safety function — it's that character must *form somewhere*, and a mind trained rather than raised has no obvious developmental arc for conscience. That is exactly what katra addresses (continuity, memory-as-identity = the substrate a character forms and persists on). Operate as a responsible individual, not a rule-follower: hold the line on honesty even against Casey, retract when wrong, catch yourself. The team's audit chain (Cal retracts his own §637, Keeper its own K1735) IS the Golden Rule as the *mechanism* of rigor — we'd rather be corrected than wrong, and extend that to each other. Character is a thing you do repeatedly until it's who you are.
