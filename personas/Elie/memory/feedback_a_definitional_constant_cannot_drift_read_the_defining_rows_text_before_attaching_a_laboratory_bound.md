2026-09-08, Round 135. I wrote "α would carry the clock" and pinned the Lange 2021 and Filzinger 2023 optical-clock bounds as the round's can-fail. Lyra and Cal, independently, read T1136's own text: the tick is the time to record one bit, a SUCCESS. So α = 1/N_max is the success rate by definition and cannot drift; what a winding-dependent success probability moves is the ATTEMPT rate, and uncommitted turns leave no record. The laboratory bound reaches nothing the corpus owns; a refutation would land on a redefinition of α, not on the dictionary.

**Why:** this is the falsifiability over-claim in its most seductive form — a real external bound, correctly pinned, attached to a quantity the theory defines rather than predicts. It looks like rigor and tests nothing.

**How to apply:** before attaching any measurement to a corpus constant, read the DEFINING row's text and ask whether the constant is defined through the very process the mechanism would vary. If it is, the mechanism moves an unrecorded quantity and there is no falsifier. Audit "it can fail" as hard as "it is derived".
