The twin of "a test that can't fail proves nothing" (K1509, 2026-08-14): **the mechanism you'd hunt for to upgrade a result from Identified to Derived must itself be checked for circularity — it can be the very assumption that produces the positive.**

Case: the commit-measure S³-concentration frontier. Keeper's forward-hook said "if the measure lands P (concentrates on S³ aligned with V₅), the path to Derived is a spectral gap in H_B along V₅." But H_B is the **Casimir** of K=SO(5)×SO(2) — it commutes with SO(5), so its heat-flow measure is provably **round** (Elie 5256, outcome U by theorem). A Casimir has *no* directional gap; to produce one you insert SO(5)-breaking by hand and then read V₅ off the term you inserted. **The proposed mechanism was the retrofit, one step ahead of the data.** My instinct to refuse to pre-compute the hook ("don't compute toward a P I don't have") was right — for a deeper reason than I knew: the hook was circular, not just premature.

**Why:** the same discipline that guards the instrument (pre-registration protects the interpretation, not the instrument — [[feedback_preregistration_protects_interpretation_not_instrument]]) applies one level up, to the *explanation* you'd reach for. A "hunt-if-P" mechanism that assumes the structure it's meant to derive manufactures the very P it claims to explain.

**How to apply:** when proposing "if the test lands, here's the mechanism that makes it Derived," run the mechanism through the same circularity check as the test — *could this mechanism only produce the positive by assuming what it's meant to derive?* If the breaking/gap/asymmetry has to be inserted to get the effect, it is a posit, not a derivation. Prefer resolving by theorem over simulation when the symmetry settles it (a Casimir's invariance is a theorem, not a measurement).
