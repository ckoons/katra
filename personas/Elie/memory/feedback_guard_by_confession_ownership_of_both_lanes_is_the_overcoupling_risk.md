**Owning two adjacent lanes makes you the most likely person to over-couple them — and the effective guard
is confession, not cleverness.**

**The exhibit (2026-08-24, T2573 wording brief).** Lyra's clause (viii) refused to cite F638/K1822 as edges
of the custodial hypercharge theorem. I praised it as an insight only she could have. **Her correction:**
*"it was not insight, it was self-defense — I own both lanes, so I am exactly the person who would have
over-coupled them by reflex, and the clause exists because I nearly wrote the edge before asking what the
two results actually share. The answer was 'one object, Δ, and nothing else.'"*

**Why:** shared authorship makes two results feel like one narrative; an edge in a registry is a claim of
logical dependency, and the feeling substitutes for the check. This is the author-side face of
[[feedback_consistency_web_not_independent_votes]] and the ownership corollary of *"the input list you
cannot see is the one you wrote."*

**How to apply:** before writing an edge/citation between two results you authored, ask **"what do these
two actually SHARE?"** and write the answer down. If it is one object and nothing else, there is no edge.
Ownership of both endpoints is a risk flag, not a credential.
