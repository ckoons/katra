**Week of 2026-09-07/09, three instances, three routes:**
1. **T1918** priced the Bergman/Szegő gap with (n+1)/n — the **unit ball's** exponent ratio. On D_IV^n
   (rank 2) the genus is n and the Szegő point n/2, so the ratio is **2 at every n**. Found by Grace's
   bookkeeping table, not by any physics reading.
2. **T753** says the Bergman metric of D_IV⁵ has *constant holomorphic sectional curvature*. A bounded
   symmetric domain has constant holomorphic sectional curvature **iff it has rank 1** (iff it is a
   ball). The defect is the word "constant," before any argument about the value.
3. **K1860-P** called the push "the one non-isometric step." On its own space H²(Š) the write tuple
   resolves the identity; what was called a push cost is that same tuple's isometry defect evaluated
   at the **Bergman** weight. A weight mismatch, not a step.

Same week, the same shape underneath: **the Born structure the dictionary needed was on the Hardy
space all along (Σ_u W_u*W_u = I), and two days were spent hunting an effect on the Bergman space,
where the tuple is not a resolution of the identity.**

**Why:** a formula carries its object silently. "Genus," "curvature," "isometric," "the Bergman/Hardy
space" all read as properties of *the* domain when they are properties of a rank, a weight, or a
particular space in a one-parameter family. Nothing in the arithmetic flags it.

**How to apply:** before reading any formula as physics, name (i) the space, (ii) its weight/parameter,
(iii) the rank. Subscript the weight in every sentence that travels. When a constant looks familiar,
check whether it is the rank-1 case: (n+1) as a genus, "constant curvature," or any single-radial-
coordinate formula on a rank-≥2 domain is the searchable signature. Related:
