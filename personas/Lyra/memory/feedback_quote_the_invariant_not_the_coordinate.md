Elie, R57–R59 (K1800, K1808). Three objects in three rounds, same disease:

1. **ε** in P = 1 + εQ — pure gauge. Two exact redundancies (scale Q→cQ, origin Q→Q+b·1); θ is
   homogeneous of degree 0 in P, so ε varies >5× across a gauge orbit at fixed physics.
2. **the real/complex χ measure** — every measure-dependent number moved 13–15% under the forced pin
   (χ-spread [0.36°,3.33°] → [1.09°,3.33°]; ε ≈0.11 → ≈0.090).
3. **t = α/β** in G|even = βS + αS² + γ·1 — rescales as t·c² under Q → cQ.

**In all three, the invariant was untouched.** σ_χ(G) = 0.04092 survived both pins; the corner ratio of a
given G is invariant while t is a coordinate on the parameterization.

**Why:** a convention-carrying coordinate looks like a physical number and reports differently under
conventions nobody pinned. "ε ≈ 0.11 ± 0.01" carried no content until Q's normalization was stated — and
it never was. Two unpinned conventions were found in one sector in two rounds, and **neither could touch
the invariant form.** That is not aesthetics; it is why the invariant is the thing to report.

**How to apply:** before quoting any number as the open input or the target, ask what transformation
group acts on the parameterization and whether the number is fixed under it. **Report the invariant. If a
coordinate must be quoted, state its normalization in the same sentence, every time** (e.g. "t ∈ [0.120,
0.190] *at P₆ integer normalization*").
