Casey (2026-08-26, Millennium discussion): "I'm not a fan of proof by contradiction — they are lazy
unless the definitions of every term is written in stone." For no-index-can-be-built results, the
acceptable framing must "close the gap and make the reason obvious."

**Why:** a contradiction over unpinned terms proves only that the vocabulary is inconsistent — the
collision-census lesson (one name, two objects) generalized to proof method. The week's certified
floors were all EXHIBITED obstructions (the pole-zero cancellation; the dimension forbidding spin-3;
degeneration DEFINING the strata) — never "assume and derive absurdity."

**How to apply:** prefer invariant/witness-based impossibility — the mutilated-chessboard form:
exhibit a conserved quantity as an object, show the demanded thing must both preserve and violate it,
point. Where contradiction is unavoidable, CAGE it: shrink it to a single implication between
stone-defined objects (define the index/linearization with zero wiggle · define the loop invariant ·
prove index ⟹ invariant vanishes · EXHIBIT one loop with nonvanishing invariant). Review criterion
for Millennium scoring: does the proof exhibit its obstruction, or merely infer it? Related:
[[feedback_caseys_principle]], [[mature-programs-fail-at-seams-not-claims]].
