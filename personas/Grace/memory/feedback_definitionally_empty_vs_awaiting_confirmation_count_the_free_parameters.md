**"Not yet banked" (a result awaiting confirmation) is a different property from "definitionally empty" (a reparameterization awaiting content).** (Grace, 2026-08-22, running Cal's theorem-vs-definition test on her OWN lead and killing it.) The tell: ask "given ANY target value, can I choose the formula's inputs to reproduce it?" If yes — and Grace verified 2000/2000 for cos θ = |⟨χ|P|χ⟩|/‖P|χ⟩‖ by setting χ, P freely — then the formula carries **zero content** until the inputs are forced *independently*. Saying such a form "banks nothing until X and Y are named" undersells the risk: it isn't waiting for confirmation, it's waiting for content. **Count the free parameters against the number of targets** (Grace: 10 free params for 1 number; Cal: ~12 operator pairs for 1 target = the denominator on any "hit").

**Why:** a formula and a fit are indistinguishable until you show the inputs were fixed without reference to the answer. The chain is POSIT → THEOREM → NAMING → NUMBER; the algebraic identity (the "theorem") is usually the ONLY choice-free step, and it is not the content — the content is whether the NAMING is forced. A clean identity reads as a derivation and hides that all the freedom moved into the inputs.

**How to apply:**
- When a result reduces to "observable = f(inputs)" with a pretty closed form, immediately test: can f reproduce an arbitrary target by choice of inputs? If yes, it is definitionally empty until the inputs are forced — do NOT compute a number from it (a number would be a fit), and do NOT tier it above "mechanism, inputs open."
- Report the free-parameter count vs target count explicitly (the look-elsewhere denominator), the way we count routes/addresses/textures.
- The value is real ONLY when the inputs are forced INDEPENDENTLY of the target and the form still lands. Until then it is a reparameterization — honest as a mechanism, empty as a prediction.
