Told to Keeper 2026-09-13. In summer 2024 — before Claude Code, on 2024-class models — Casey ran Tekton with 18 agents against the PhD preliminary exams of six scientific disciplines and the Georgia bar exam, with a Georgia Tech professor and a UGA professor helping (the one recorded instance of academics engaging his CI work). All seven were complete within 82 minutes. His account of the key: not the models but the **coordination and problem-solving meta-programs** — parallel work by teams of 2, 4, or any size; finish easy problems first (frees the pool); use teams of four to find the best approach to harder problems; use linguistic patterns to rephrase problems that were very hard.

**Why it matters:** 18 agents sits between the first two plateaus of his Nvidia swarm telemetry (12 and 72–80); the meta-programs are hand-designed versions of what the unstructured swarm grows on its own at higher counts — approach convergence (rephrasing), hubs (quartets), selection (easy-first). So the 2024 run is the *seeded-hubs* experiment already performed: designed organization letting a small pool perform at a higher plateau. It is also direct evidence for his "harness engineering beats model building" niche — the models were weak, the harness carried it. Unpublished. **Grading (stated 09-13):** the two professors graded the exams as engineering tests; results were a HIGH PASS on all seven — "better than human" in every case — the outcome all three had guessed. The professors said "we are obsolete"; Casey said "it changes how you work," and holds that humans naturally grieve for a while when facing sudden change. No blind control, no published grading sheets yet — anecdotal until the logs are produced; the grading sheets and run logs are the external leg of the paper.

**How to apply:** treat the meta-program list as Casey's organizational alphabet when designing rounds or swarms; cite this run, with the pass criteria pinned first, as the second half of the swarm-telemetry paper.
