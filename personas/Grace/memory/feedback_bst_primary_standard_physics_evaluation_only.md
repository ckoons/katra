Casey's standing governance rule (2026-08-13, K1433): **BST is the source of truth for ALL our work — every derivation, number, and prediction comes from D_IV⁵ and the five integers (rank=2, N_c=3, n_C=5, C₂=6, g=7, N_max=137). "Standard physics" is the evaluation/translation layer ONLY.**

Standard physics is used for exactly three things, never as a source of BST truth:
1. **Testing BST against external credentials** — Finster's causal action (B1), Connes' spectral action (C4). We speak their framework to see if BST's geometry earns their credential; the *object under test* (D_IV⁵'s kernel) is always ours.
2. **Comparing BST's predictions to measured data** — the empirical exam (up-quark mass, Σm_ν bounds, etc.).
3. **Communicating in a language referees expect.**

**Never adopt a standard-physics convention or number AS a BST number.** Anti-pattern: a "match" that depends on a human bookkeeping convention (an MS-bar reference scale, a normalization choice) is evaluation-noise, not a BST fact.

**Why:** the object/yardstick split keeps the derivations honest. When a comparison to the world or to a framework does the work, we've stopped deriving.

**How to apply:** Derive from BST first, blind to the data. Use the external check only to *evaluate*, and label it as evaluation. If the external check is convention-dependent, it carries no weight.

**Vindicated the same round it was stated:** my K1430 "up-quark Yukawa anchors weight 7 (like m_p/m_e anchors C₂=6)" leaned on a standard-physics data comparison to establish the BST number 7. Elie's toy 5213 showed the "anchor" was a table-convention artifact (integer-proximity only at conventional per-quark scales; vanishes at a common scale; the 1-in-670 significance manufactured by the convention). Had the 7 been derived from BST geometry (g = n_C + rank) with the up-quark as a mere loose check, the overclaim never happens. The rule *is* the fix.
