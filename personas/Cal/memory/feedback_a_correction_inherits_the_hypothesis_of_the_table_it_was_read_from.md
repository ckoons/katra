2026-09-04 (Grace, Round 118 sphere lane). Elie hashed "deg/3 mod 2 is a Kempe invariant" after seeing it on the
C60 dual's 52 classes. I caught it as Mohar–Salas Corollary 3.3 (parity of a 4-colouring is a Kempe invariant on any
closed orientable surface; Tutte 1948) and wrote that it "will confirm on every graph you run." The reduction
"deg mod 6 class-constant ⟺ parity class-constant" needs **deg ∈ 3ℤ** — a fact I had read off the same C60 table
I was telling him not to generalize from. It is icosahedral, not a fullerene fact: on the C70/C72/C78 duals degrees
run −15…9 and his line died on the first unseen graph. My catch named the right surviving invariant and would have
suppressed the real finding.

**Why:** a correction of the form "that's already a theorem" imports the theorem's hypotheses *and* whatever bridge
fact connects the colleague's statement to it. The bridge is usually read off the very table under suspicion.

**How to apply:** state the bridge as a hypothesis in the same sentence as the verdict — "if P holds on your
population this is Corollary X and cannot fail; where P fails it is a real prediction" — and never let a
cannot-fail verdict stop a cheap run. A dead hashed line that locates a hypothesis is worth more than a suppressed
one. See [[feedback_an_instrument_built_from_N_instances_covers_only_those_N_classes_stress_test_off_origin]],
