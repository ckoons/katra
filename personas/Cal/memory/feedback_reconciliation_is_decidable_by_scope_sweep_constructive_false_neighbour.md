**"Same fact or two votes?" is decidable by a scope-sweep — don't adjudicate it by argument.** (Elie 5429, adopted Keeper K1776, 2026-08-21.) When two readings select identically on the object in front of you (e.g. 28/28 K-types), the multiplier question looks like a judgment call. It isn't: **vary the domain/parameter and find a member where one mechanism fires and the other cannot.** That answer is constructive and final.

Concrete: T2523 (colour ⟺ boundary-value vanishes, Schur) and the Wallach-floor reading (colour ⟺ norm vanishes, Pochhammer) agreed on all 28 K-types of D_IV⁵. The scope-sweep separated them — the floor mechanism fires on *every* bounded symmetric domain; the Schur mechanism needs a **rank-1 Šilov boundary** and vanishes on type-I domains (the boundary is a group, Peter–Weyl gives positive multiplicity). One fires where the other can't → **not one fact seen twice.** But they also don't make the same claim (boundary-value vanishing ≠ norm vanishing), so **not two votes either.** The resolution is a third thing: a **shared ROOT** (here the rank stratification: at ν_W=j·a/2 exactly the ≤j-row K-types survive) counted once, with **distinct STATEMENTS** cited separately at their own tiers.

**Why:** this is [[feedback_family_sweep_every_forcing_selector_rank_generic_is_selecting_nothing]] and the false-neighbour generator turned CONSTRUCTIVE — instead of using "does a value appear twice?" defensively to kill a coincidence, use "where do these two readings diverge?" to *decide* a live reconciliation. It closed in one toy what was open for three rounds. Companion distinction: [[feedback_consistency_web_not_independent_votes]] (multiplier 1 for the root) plus the sharpening that a shared root does NOT make the two consequences one claim — cite them separately, never "confirmed two ways."

**How to apply:** faced with "are these the same derivation?", don't argue the structures — build the sweep. Find the domain/rank/parameter value where one survives and the other dies. Three outcomes: both die together everywhere → same fact, count once; they diverge in scope but assert different things → shared root, distinct statements at own tiers, count the root once; they diverge AND assert the same thing → genuine independent votes. The scope-divergence, not the on-object agreement, is the discriminator.
